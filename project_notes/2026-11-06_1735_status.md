# 2026-11-06 17:35 — Cisco Desk Camera green/broken through the P49w-30: FIXED

**Root cause.** Through the MacBook → OWC TB5 hub → Thunderbolt → P49w-30 path, the monitor's hub has a real USB3 link,
so the camera enumerated at 5 Gb/s and streamed uncompressed NV12. The monitor's USB3 hub chain (Intel `8087:0b40` → Genesys
`17ef:108f` → `17ef:1091`) drops its isochronous packets:
`AppleUSBXHCIIsochronousRequest ... (Cisco Desk Camera 1080p@00222340) endpoint 0x81: status 0xe00002ed (transaction error)`,
~200 errors per 10 s and about 10% of frames delivered, at any resolution (360p too). Lost packets leave zeroed NV12 data, which shows as **green**.
The lead hypothesis in the brief (DP alt mode forces USB 2.0) was wrong: the link was 5 Gb/s both direct and through the monitor.
Lighting is not the cause (light on: 16 frames, 242 errors).

**Evidence (camtest, 10 s each).** Through the monitor at 5 Gb/s: 15–16 frames, 187–242 errors. Direct at 5 Gb/s: 150 frames, 0 errors.
Through the monitor with a USB 2.0 cable at 480 Mb/s: 286 frames, 0 errors (MJPEG).

**Fix (tested by Dawid 17:34; accepted 17:37: "now it works, thanks").** USB 2.0-only cable between the camera and the monitor, in the same monitor port.
Nothing changed in software, drivers or monitor settings.

**Why it worked before.** On 31.08 the camera sat in the monitor's USB 2.0 tree (GUIDE.md); this is inferred, no speed was recorded then.
macOS 27 (26.09) is also new since then; it is not provable from the logs.

**Open (optional).** Lenovo monitor firmware could fix SuperSpeed isochronous; not needed now. Retest with `rpi/camera-via-monitor-2026-11-06/camtest`.
Do not give the camera a USB3 cable on the monitor again.
